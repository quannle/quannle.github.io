On AI Persuasion.

There are some truly sci-fi sentiments from the canon of catastrophic AI narratives that proclaim that the advent of AGI/ASI will bring about agents that can convince anyone of anything. While I'm sure there are those who still hold this belief, it seems that the more relevant concerns are those regarding today's AI models: the current and potential impacts of widely-used, scalable, text/image/video generation models.

I work on a very particular kind of persuasion: political persuasion from actors with defined agendas and desired outcomes (e.g. the election of a particular candidate, or the support of a particular policy). It's not hard to see that this setting isn't the only one of concern. When twitter/X users comment "@grok is this real?", or ask "@grok what does this mean?", or otherwise turn to AI for answers, many users are completely unaware of the system prompt contents, or even the extent that the system prompt can affect the results. It's easy to pick on Grok for its very public failures: from praising Hitler to asserting that Elon is actually the best at every conceivable task.

But when people come to AI for serious life decisions, how should it respond? How does that response differ from the counterfactual world where the user doesn't use AI to make that decision? I suppose when we're at this framing we've left persuasion to consider the broader problem of societal impacts. I'm concerned about the implicit persuasion that happens despite the alignment process. Ideally alignment is able to bake certain sensible defaults, but there seems to be a lot of gaps -- people have committed serious harm to themselves and others after interacting with AI.

I think fixing those gaps should involve closing the loop -- connecting impacts research with the pretraining/posttraining/alignment process. Frontier labs spend a lot of time aligning the model and crafting prompts, systems, and harnesses, but how much of time is spent using actual impacts to steer development?
